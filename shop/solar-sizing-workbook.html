Who it is for

People planning a stand-alone (off-grid) solar system for a cabin, homestead, workshop, tiny house or RV, who want to understand the numbers before comparing kits or talking to an installer. It suits a first system and a careful upgrade alike. You do not need to be an electrician, but you do need to be comfortable typing numbers into a spreadsheet.

It is a planning tool, not a design service. The finished design, protection and installation must follow your equipment manuals and local electrical code, and should be checked by a qualified, licensed electrician where the law or the risk requires it.

What the workbook calculates

1. Load audit

Up to 40 appliances: daily energy, essential-only energy, inverter standby, simultaneous AC load and the peak while a motor starts. Measured readings override estimates.

2. Monthly sun hours

Twelve months of peak sun hours from PVWatts, PVGIS or Global Solar Atlas. Pick the months the system must cover; see the energy balance for every month.

3. Solar array

Array watts from the design month, losses and reserve, rounded to whole panels, with the energy actually delivered in the worst month.

4. Battery bank

Required watt-hours and amp-hours, then a series and parallel layout for the batteries you are considering, and the days of storage it really gives.

5. Inverter

Continuous and surge targets with pass/check against a chosen model, plus the DC current it can draw from the battery.

6. Charge controller

Charge current at full array output, cold-morning string voltage against the controller's limit, and PV current checks.

7. Cable voltage drop

Smallest AWG meeting your drop allowance for each DC run, with a metric mm² minimum. Ampacity and protection are flagged as separate checks.

8. Summary

A one-page specification and every check in one place, ready to compare against kits and quotes.

How it differs from our free calculator

The free solar sizing calculator gives a quick first estimate from a single daily energy figure. The workbook builds that figure from an itemised audit, checks every month instead of one, lays out real batteries, checks inverter surge and DC current, catches cold-weather controller overvoltage and sizes cable for voltage drop. It also saves your project so you can revisit it when loads or prices change.

Limits, stated plainly

  • It is an energy-balance planning tool. It does not model weather sequences, shading, battery ageing, temperature derating or recharge time after a flat battery.
  • Default efficiencies and margins are editable assumptions, not equipment ratings. Replace them with your data-sheet figures.
  • The cable sheet checks voltage drop only. Ampacity, insulation rating, overcurrent protection, disconnects and grounding must be sized separately from code tables and manufacturer instructions.
  • It does not cover grid-tied or hybrid grid systems, generator sizing or wind and hydro generation.

Questions

Do I need Excel?

No. The workbook uses standard functions that work in Google Sheets and the free LibreOffice Calc.

Is this suitable outside the United States?

The sizing method works anywhere; sun-hour sources cover most of the world. References to the National Electrical Code apply to the US. Elsewhere, follow your own wiring rules.

Will it tell me which brand to buy?

No. It produces a specification and a checklist so you can judge any kit or quote yourself. We do not sell equipment.

Can I see the method before buying?

Yes. Our free guides cover the power audit and sizing method, and the free worksheet includes the load-audit pages from the guide.
