Off-Grid Water: Collect, Store & Filter

Water is your most critical off-grid system and the most commonly underestimated. We cover rainwater harvesting, gravity-fed distribution, well basics, and filtration that reliably produces safe drinking water.

Choosing Your Off-Grid Water Source

Most off-grid properties have more than one viable water source. The right choice depends on your land, rainfall, and budget — and many homesteaders use a combination. Here’s how they compare:

Rainwater catchment is the starting point for most homesteaders because it requires no drilling or permits in most states, scales with your roof area, and produces soft water that’s easy to filter. Calculate Water Storage from Your Own Demand

Start with measured daily use, then choose the period you need to bridge between dependable replenishments. Separate drinking and cooking, hygiene, livestock and irrigation so you can see what can be reduced during a shortage.

Usable storage required = Daily demand × Days without replenishment. Add separately chosen reserves and account for water that cannot be drawn from the bottom of a tank.

For arithmetic only: a household using 40 gallons per day needs 2,400 usable gallons for 60 days before any extra reserve. This is an illustrative input, not a recommended household allowance. At a planning allowance of 1 gallon per person per day, four people use 120 gallons in a 30-day month; at 2 gallons each, they use 240 gallons.

Check whether your catchment or well can refill the storage through the season. Tank size alone does not establish a reliable or drinkable supply. See the water storage guide for further planning considerations.

Filtration: Match Treatment to the Source

Test first, then select equipment for the identified contaminants. A sediment filter protects downstream equipment; a gravity-fed container does not by itself establish microbial or chemical treatment capability.

Use the water filtration guide to compare treatment methods and check the exact system’s documentation. Include operating conditions, maintenance, treated-water storage and backup supply in the plan.

Water FAQ

How much water storage do I actually need?

Multiply measured daily demand by the period without dependable replenishment, then add your chosen reserves and allow for unusable tank volume. For example, 40 gallons per day over 60 days requires 2,400 usable gallons before reserves. Add irrigation and livestock demand if those uses must continue.

Is rainwater safe to drink after filtering?

Collected rainwater is not automatically drinkable. Ask about testing and treatment for the intended use; a first-flush diverter does not establish safety. See CDC rainwater guidance and our treatment selection guide.

Do I need a pump for an off-grid water system?

Not necessarily. If you can position your storage tank uphill from your home (even 10–15 feet of elevation), gravity provides 4–7 PSI — adequate for a sink, outdoor shower, and garden drip irrigation without any pump or electricity. For a full indoor shower pressure (typically 30+ PSI), you need either much more elevation (70+ feet) or a pump. A 12V DC pump running on your solar system is the most efficient option when gravity pressure isn’t sufficient.

Is rainwater harvesting legal where I am?

In most US states, yes — and the trend has been toward legalization everywhere. A handful of states (historically Colorado and Utah) had restrictions, but most have since relaxed them. Some states require permits for large systems (>5,000 gallons). Check your specific state’s current statutes before building. Our rainwater harvesting guide has a state-by-state legality overview.
