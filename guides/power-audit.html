How to Do a Power Audit Before Buying Solar

Difficulty: Beginner | Reading time: ~6 min

By The Off-Grid Workshop

In This Guide

Keep Energy and Power Separate

A power audit should produce a daily energy budget and a separate record of running and startup power. Daily energy helps size generation and storage. Simultaneous AC loads and startup requirements help select an inverter.

Watts (W) describe power at an instant. Watt-hours (Wh) describe energy over time; 1,000Wh equals 1kWh. An illustrative 400W load used for 15 minutes consumes 400 × 0.25 = 100Wh. A different 100W load used for one hour also consumes 100Wh, but the two loads impose different power requirements.

List the Loads and Their Operating Conditions

Include lights, refrigeration, communications, pumps, fans, chargers, tools and occasional high-power appliances. Record quantities, whether each load is AC or DC, and whether it must operate during a shortage.

Make separate lists for seasonal or unusually busy days. A winter heating load, summer fan or occasional workshop session can disappear in an annual average. Keep an evidence column so measured values, manufacturer ratings and estimates remain distinguishable.

Measure Energy Where You Can

Virginia Cooperative Extension explains the watts-times-hours method for estimating energy use. For a steady load, multiply its input watts by its daily operating hours, including quantity when needed.

For cycling equipment, a suitable energy meter can capture energy over an observation period rather than assuming the device runs at its rated power for a fixed share of the day. Use equipment within its stated ratings and follow its instructions. Ask a qualified person about loads that cannot be safely measured with an appropriate plug-in meter.

Daily Wh = Recorded kWh × 1,000 × 24 ÷ Observation hours. For an illustrative refrigerator reading of 0.8kWh over 48 hours, the average is 400Wh/day. Record conditions and repeat when they change; one interval does not establish every season’s demand.

Record inverter standby consumption separately for the system design. Note where each measurement was taken so you do not count the same energy or conversion loss twice.

Do Not Confuse AC Volts-Amps with Watts

For a single-phase AC load, RMS volts multiplied by RMS amps gives apparent power in volt-amperes (VA). Real input power in watts also depends on power factor. Yokogawa describes the relationship as W = V × A × power factor.

A label reading 120V and 1.5A therefore gives 180VA, not necessarily 180W. With an illustrative power factor of 0.8, the real power would be 144W. Use measured input watts or appropriate manufacturer data when estimating energy. Do not substitute a motor’s mechanical horsepower rating for its electrical input.

Keep a second record for simultaneous AC running loads and each relevant startup event. Include the other loads that may remain on while a motor starts.

Record Startup Power and Duration Separately

Victron’s Wiring Unlimited explains AC system current, VA and watts. Check the inverter’s applicable continuous W/VA limits and the equipment’s starting requirements. A daily Wh total cannot establish those limits.

For example, a refrigerator’s measured 400Wh/day does not tell you its compressor’s running watts or startup current. Obtain the relevant specifications or suitable measurements. For a startup event, check the required duration as well as the peak, together with battery/BMS and wiring limits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate daily energy use?

For a steady load, multiply input watts by hours per day and quantity. For a measured interval, multiply recorded kWh by 1,000 and by 24 divided by observation hours. Sum the daily results without double-counting.

Does daily Wh tell me what inverter to buy?

No. Record simultaneous AC running loads and startup requirements separately, including applicable watts, VA and startup duration.

Is volts multiplied by amps always watts?

For single-phase AC, RMS volts multiplied by RMS amps gives VA. Real watts also depend on power factor. Use appropriate input-power data for energy estimates.

Should I use a typical appliance table instead of measuring?

Use generic estimates only as identified placeholders. Replace them with data for the actual equipment and operating conditions before committing to a system design.

Take the Audit into System Sizing

Keep the appliance-energy total, operating conditions and overhead notes together. Use the solar sizing calculator to explore array and nominal-storage requirements, then review the calculation’s combined loss allowance and storage assumptions. Enter simultaneous AC running load and startup load in their separate fields.

The calculator is a preliminary estimate. Use the audit record to discuss seasonal demand, standby use, conversion losses, backup charging and equipment ratings with the system designer. Daily Wh alone does not establish the project’s installed cost.
