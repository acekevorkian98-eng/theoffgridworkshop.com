LiFePO4 vs Lead-Acid: Which Battery for Off-Grid Solar?

Compare Actual Batteries, Not Chemistry Labels Alone

Choose a battery bank around the energy and power your system needs, the installation environment, compatible charging equipment and the maintenance you can provide. LiFePO4, flooded lead-acid, AGM and gel products do not share one set of operating limits.

Collect the exact model datasheet, installation manual and warranty before comparing prices. Record nominal voltage, rated capacity and test conditions, permitted discharge, charge/discharge current, temperature limits, protection requirements and approved bank configurations.

Calculate Usable Energy in Watt-Hours

Approximate nominal energy (Wh) = nominal battery voltage (V) × rated capacity (Ah). Amp-hours alone do not describe energy. Use nominal voltage rather than the charger’s absorption setting.

Illustrative load-side energy = nominal Wh × selected discharge fraction × battery-to-load efficiency. The following examples demonstrate the arithmetic, not product performance or recommended operating limits:

Illustrative assumptions, before additional temperature, ageing or reserve allowances
Example BankNominal EnergyAssumed Discharge / Delivery EfficiencyEstimated Energy at Loads
25.6 V, 100 Ah2,560 Wh80% / 90%1,843.2 Wh
24 V, 200 Ah4,800 Wh50% / 85%2,040 Wh

These banks are not equivalent in delivered energy. For a 2,400 Wh load-side target using the first example’s assumptions, the nominal-energy requirement is 2,400 ÷ (0.80 × 0.90) = about 3,333 Wh, before further design allowances. Check current and startup-power requirements separately.

For accounting, two 12.8 V, 100 Ah batteries contain approximately 2,560 Wh in total. An approved series configuration would be 25.6 V at 100 Ah; an approved parallel configuration would be 12.8 V at 200 Ah. Neither arrangement creates extra energy. This does not establish that a particular battery permits either connection; follow its bank-configuration limits.

Read Cycle Ratings with Their Conditions

A cycle count needs a stated discharge depth, test conditions and remaining-capacity threshold. It is not a guaranteed calendar lifetime. For example, Victron’s Lithium Smart data lists 2,500 cycles at 80% depth of discharge and 5,000 at 50%, with capacity at least 80% of nominal. Those figures describe that product family, not every LiFePO4 battery.

Compare like-for-like conditions and inspect the warranty separately. Do not convert a laboratory cycle count directly into promised years on your site. Temperature, operating pattern and charging affect service life; Trojan likewise describes life expectancy as dependent on the application.

Match Chargers, Loads and Protection

Use charge voltage, current limits and control behavior specified for the exact battery. Confirm every charging source, including solar, mains, generator and alternator equipment. A chemistry preset or the words “lithium compatible” are not a substitute for checking the actual profile and protection interface.

PWM versus MPPT is not itself a lithium-compatibility rule. Renogy lists lithium among the supported battery types for its Wanderer Li 30A PWM controller. That is evidence against a blanket ban on PWM, not a recommendation that this controller fits every battery or PV array.

A BMS does not replace correctly designed cables, fuses, isolation or compatible system controls. Some batteries require a separate BMS. Victron’s installation instructions cover its BMS connections, cable/fuse ratings and permitted configurations. Use the instructions for your own equipment and have the installation checked against applicable requirements.

Check Battery Temperature Before Charging

Use the battery manufacturer’s charging-temperature range, sensing method and charge-inhibit instructions. A generic “above freezing is safe” rule is inadequate. The Lithium Smart family cited above specifies charging from +5°C to +50°C, while its discharge range is different. Those are product-specific limits, not settings for another battery.

Check that low-temperature protection is actually present and that every charging source responds to it. Do not assume the word BMS guarantees all protections or overrides the manual. For a heated battery, verify heater operation, energy demand and the conditions under which cell charging can resume.

Insulation slows heat transfer; it does not generate heat or prove that cells are warm enough to charge. Include any required enclosure heating in the power audit. Lead-acid also needs its own temperature-related charging and storage provisions; it is not an unrestricted cold-weather substitute.

Plan Maintenance and Storage

Flooded lead-acid requires electrolyte and charging care. Sealed AGM/gel and LiFePO4 avoid routine electrolyte topping-up, but they still need inspection, compatible settings and appropriate storage. “No watering” does not mean the complete battery system needs no attention.

Trojan’s maintenance guidance distinguishes flooded and VRLA products, describes ventilation during charging, and provides different charging instructions. Do not transfer a flooded-battery equalization routine to another battery type. Follow the exact model instructions rather than copying a generic voltage or maintenance interval.

Record inspection and storage requirements, protective-device behavior and who can service the system. For a model-specific example, consult Victron’s operation and care instructions. In a used bank, verify condition and compatibility rather than assuming a low purchase price means useful remaining capacity.

Compare Costs with Explicit Scenarios

No chemistry wins every ten-year cost comparison. Use current quotes for banks that meet the same load-side energy, power and environmental requirements. Include compatible chargers/BMS, enclosure and support, heating or ventilation, installation, maintenance and end-of-life handling.

Calculate more than one replacement scenario instead of assuming a fixed number of replacements. State which lifetimes and prices are assumptions, which come from a warranty, and which have operating evidence. A warranty period is not proof that a bank will deliver its original capacity throughout that period.

Choose after checking the complete system, not solely the purchase price per nominal Ah. The solar sizing calculator can help expose energy assumptions; it does not approve a battery model or installation.

Common Comparison Questions

Is LiFePO4 always cheaper over ten years?

No. Compare actual banks meeting the same requirements, with current quotes and explicit lifetime, replacement, maintenance and installation assumptions.

Does a cycle rating guarantee a number of years?

No. Read the rating’s discharge depth, conditions and remaining-capacity threshold, and assess the warranty separately. Site operation can differ from the test.

Can every LiFePO4 battery charge above 0 degrees Celsius?

No universal threshold applies. Follow the exact battery’s charging-temperature range and protection instructions. The cited Victron Lithium Smart family specifies +5 to +50 degrees Celsius for charging.

Can a PWM controller charge lithium batteries?

Some PWM models support lithium charging. Verify the particular controller profile, battery requirements, temperature protection and PV limits; PWM or MPPT alone does not establish compatibility.
