When and How to Clean Solar Panels

Start with the Module Manual

A cleaning plan needs three inputs: the instructions for the installed modules, the deposits actually present and a safe way to reach them. A calendar reminder is useful for inspection, but it does not establish that every array needs an annual wash.

Find the model and applicable manual in the installation records. Ask the installer if they are missing. Check approved equipment, chemicals, water quality, pressure limits and warranty conditions before choosing a method.

Why Cleaning Needs Vary

Dust, soot and organic deposits can reduce the light reaching the cells. The effect depends on the deposit and how much has accumulated. Rain removes some kinds of soiling; others need washing. Sandia notes that nearby pollution sources and local conditions can produce different losses even within one installation. See Sandia's explanation of soiling losses.

Canadian Solar's maintenance FAQ says sufficiently tilted modules may benefit from rain cleaning, while high-soiling projects need more maintenance. That is conditional guidance for its products, not a promise that rain will clean every array.

Check Safety Before Cleaning

Canadian Solar's module manual, sections 2 and 8, warns that illuminated modules remain electrically hazardous when disconnected. Switching off an inverter does not remove that hazard. Follow the documented isolation procedure; leave electrical work to qualified personnel. Do not unplug connectors to improvise a shutdown.

Stop for cracked glass, damaged wiring or electrical faults; wet damage increases shock risk. Arrange competent help if the roof cannot be reached with suitable access and fall protection.

The Trina Solar Vertex manual, cleaning section, also prohibits stepping on modules and directing water onto module backs or cables, and calls for keeping connectors dry. Follow the instructions for your own installation rather than transferring another manufacturer's procedure.

Use Approved Materials and Methods

For its covered modules, Canadian Solar specifies gentle, non-abrasive implements, low-mineral water and avoiding the hottest hours. It forbids scraping dry dirt and restricts chemicals to approved substances. Those requirements do not establish a universal dish-soap recipe or pressure-washer setting.

If deposits remain, ask the module supplier about a compatible treatment. Do not escalate to a scraper, solvent or powered brush based on generic advice. Check the applicable warranty before hiring a cleaner, and ask them to identify the method they intend to use.

Build an Inspection and Service Record

Use this planning checklist with the installer or maintenance provider:

  1. Record the module model, manual revision, service contact and safe access arrangements.
  2. Note visible deposits, recent dusty work, rainfall and any system alarms from a safe observation point.
  3. Agree whether the next action is observation, approved cleaning or fault investigation.
  4. After service, record the date, method, water or cleaning agent used and any defects reported.
  5. Set the next inspection around the site's conditions and the manufacturer's maintenance requirements.

Assess the Value of Cleaning

The NREL operation and maintenance guide, cleaning discussion, recommends a site-specific cost-benefit assessment for uniform soiling. Cleaning cost, soiling rate, solar resource and the value of delivered energy affect the decision. Its examples are not a forecast for an individual homestead.

For an off-grid system, separate potential array production from energy actually accepted by the loads or battery. A Victron controller troubleshooting guide lists a full battery, weather, shading and charging limits among reasons for low output. A lower daily total alone does not diagnose dirt.

As an illustrative decision calculation, 20 kWh of additional usable energy valued at 0.30 currency units per kWh is worth 6 currency units. The 20 kWh is an assumed recovery, not a measured result or prediction. Include service and water costs, and keep safety or defect work separate from this energy-only calculation.

Common Questions

Is cleaning once a year enough?

There is no universal interval. Use the module manual, local soiling, rainfall and inspection records to decide when cleaning is needed.

Does rain clean solar panels?

Rain can remove some deposits, but it does not remove every type of soiling. Check the actual array rather than assuming rainfall has cleaned it.

Can I use dish soap or a pressure washer?

Check the exact module cleaning instructions first. Do not assume a household detergent, pressure setting or cleaning tool is approved for every module.

Does switching off the inverter make the panels safe to touch?

No. Illuminated modules can remain electrically hazardous. Follow the documented system procedure and use qualified personnel for electrical work.
