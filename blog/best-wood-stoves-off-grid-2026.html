Wood Stove Buying Guide: Ratings, Listings and Site Fit

Check model identity, intended use, published ratings and complete installation costs before choosing a wood stove for an off-grid home.

Compare Evidence Before Comparing Stoves

Choose for the building and its permitted use, then compare the complete installation and operating requirements. The manufacturer examples below were checked on October 24, 2026. They illustrate listing checks, not a ranked shortlist or independently measured performance.

Keep the model number, market, manual revision, certification information and dated quotation together. A familiar product name can refer to different versions or an older appliance.

Drolet HT-3000: Check Status and Rating Basis

The manufacturer's DB07300 page identifies the HT-3000 as the successor to the HT2000 and is marked discontinued. It remains useful as a specification example, but it does not establish current stock or make the older HT2000 the same model.

Published Figures Are Not All the Same Measure

The page lists average dry-cordwood overall efficiency as 71 percent HHV and 76 percent LHV. It separately lists maximum dry-cordwood output at 110,000 BTU/h and maximum overall output rate at 57,800 BTU/h. Its footnotes describe different fuel-loading bases. Do not compare a headline maximum from one listing with a test-rate figure from another as if they were equivalent.

Cubic Mini: Residential Suitability Comes First

Cubic Mini's FAQ states that its stoves are not approved for residential installation and do not have mobile-home certification. A small floor area does not turn that product into a suitable residential-cabin recommendation.

Check the Exact Model and Type of Certification

The manufacturer distinguishes non-certified Cub CB-1000 and Grizzly CB-2000 units from safety-certified CB-1008 and CB-1210 units. It also distinguishes safety certification from environmental and mobile-home certification. Do not transfer a manual, shielding rule or chimney kit between those groups.

Ashford 30.2: Keep Claims Attributed

Blaze King's Ashford 30.2 page publishes a 2.9 cubic foot firebox and a 6-inch flue size, with a link to its manual. Those specifications belong to that named appliance, not every Blaze King stove.

Output and Duration Need Their Conditions

The page reports constant high output of 37,742 BTU/h for ten hours and low output of 10,641 BTU/h for up to thirty hours. These are manufacturer-published figures, not results measured by The Off-Grid Workshop or a guarantee of room temperature in a particular home. Ask how the published test conditions relate to the proposed use.

Used or Older Stoves Need More Than a Low Price

For a used appliance, obtain the identification plate, correct manual, condition assessment, parts availability and installation acceptance before committing. Include any chimney work and missing components in the quote.

Local sale and installation rules matter. Washington Ecology, for example, regulates the sale and transfer of wood-burning devices and directs installation questions to the local building permit department. Check the applicable authority for the actual property.

Match the Building Heat Loss

Have the proposed heating system assessed against the building's heat loss, winter conditions, layout and desired temperatures. Include how heat reaches separate rooms and what happens when nobody is available to load fuel.

There is no universal stove output for a given cabin floor area. Insulation, air leakage, glazing and climate change the load. Record the assumptions behind a sizing proposal rather than adding an arbitrary percentage to a chart.

Price the Installed System and Support

Request a quote covering the appliance, delivery, floor and hearth work, connector, chimney or liner, roof or wall penetrations, supports, air supply, alarms, approvals and commissioning. Resolve exclusions and responsibility for inspection.

Ask about replacement parts, service access, fuel supply and any electrical accessories. Keep operating and maintenance costs separate from the purchase total. Availability, delivered price and local acceptance need confirmation for the actual order.

Make the Selection from a Complete Proposal

Select only after the appliance's intended use, building requirements, installation design and full cost are resolved. Keep the accepted proposal and manufacturer instructions for handover.

A product's small size, long advertised burn or high efficiency percentage cannot settle those decisions alone. Use the companion installation guide to review the remaining project scope.

Common Questions

Is the Cubic Mini Cub suitable for a residential cabin?

The manufacturer states that its stoves are not approved for residential installation. Do not select one for a dwelling based on its small size or a safety-certification label.

Does a published heating area tell me the right stove size?

No. Match the building heat loss, layout, climate and operating needs to the specific appliance. Manufacturer area estimates are not a building assessment.

Can I compare any two efficiency percentages?

Check the basis and test conditions first. HHV, LHV and optimum figures are different measures and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Does a discontinued model page prove a stove is available?

No. Check the exact model, supplier stock, support, condition and installation acceptance before paying for it.
