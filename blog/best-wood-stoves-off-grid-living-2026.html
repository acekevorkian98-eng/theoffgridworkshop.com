Wood Stove Installation and Care: Planning Guide

Plan a wood-stove installation with the correct documents, venting, floor protection, inspections, fuel, alarms and maintenance.

Plan the Stove and Venting as One System

The appliance, connector, chimney, air supply, hearth and building need to work together. EPA's archived Burn Wise FAQ recommends professional installation and explains the importance of the venting system to draft and performance. The programme stopped updating in October 2025; use it as background, alongside current product instructions and local requirements.

This guide is a project and care checklist. It does not provide framing dimensions, clearance reductions or instructions for a specific chimney assembly.

Collect the Documents Before Work Starts

Identify the exact appliance and retain its rating plate, serial number, manual and applicable installation drawings. Obtain instructions for every venting component and accessory in the proposed system.

Manufacturer resources can be model-specific: Drolet's DB07300 document page separates installation, safety and dimension documents. Confirm that the selected manual applies to the actual unit. A brochure or another model's diagram is not a substitute.

Specify Fuel and Check Moisture

Use only the fuel allowed by the appliance manual. EPA's archived wood-burning guidance recommends wood below 20 percent moisture and checking it with a moisture meter. Do not replace a moisture check with a fixed number of months in a stack.

The same source warns against painted or pressure-treated wood, glued wood products and household rubbish. A species ranking alone does not describe the usable heat from the fuel actually available.

Questions for the Installation Review

Appliance Suitability

Does the exact model permit the proposed use, building type and location? Check the listing and local acceptance before delivery.

Floor and Hearth

Has the floor been assessed for the installed load? Is the required floor protection specified, including its construction and extent?

Clearances and Shielding

Are appliance, connector and chimney clearances shown separately? Check the allowed assembly rather than assuming that a shield permits a standard reduction.

Chimney Route

Do the drawings cover the complete route, supports, penetrations, termination and inspection access? Have existing chimney condition and compatibility been assessed?

Air Supply and Extraction

Has the installer considered combustion air and interaction with extract fans or other equipment in the building?

Components and Substitutions

Are the selected components compatible under their instructions? Resolve substitutions before work rather than mixing parts on site.

Inspection and Commissioning

Who checks the completed work, records any conditions and demonstrates operation before routine use?

Service Access

Can the stove, connector and chimney be inspected and maintained without dismantling unrelated building work?

Resolve Heat Delivery and Backup

Agree the intended heated area and comfort goal with the designer. Include room separation, climate, building heat loss and periods when the stove will not be tended.

If the plan relies on a blower or other powered accessory, document that load and the expected effect of a power failure. Keep a suitable alternative plan for interruptions instead of treating a long burn-time claim as guaranteed heating.

Confirm Approval and Complete the Handover

Washington Ecology directs wood-stove installation questions to the local building permit department. Other locations have their own authorities and requirements. Confirm the rules for the property rather than importing a permit exemption from elsewhere.

Retain the approved scope, completed-work record, inspection outcome and product instructions. Have the installer demonstrate lighting, loading, controls, normal shutdown and the response to abnormal operation for this appliance.

Set an Inspection and Cleaning Schedule

The US Fire Administration advises professional cleaning and inspection of heating equipment and chimneys each year. The Oregon State Fire Marshal describes at least annual professional chimney and vent inspection and cleaning. Neither statement guarantees that an annual visit alone is sufficient under every operating condition.

Follow the manual and servicing advice for additional checks, cleaning and replacement of worn parts. Record the work, findings and next inspection. Do not assume that a non-catalytic stove needs no care beyond emptying ash.

Respond to Smoke, Deposits and Alarms

EPA's archived FAQ explains that newer stoves still require professional inspection and cleaning. Do not substitute a homemade deposit colour chart or a temperature rule for an assessment of the complete appliance and flue.

CPSC's alarm guidance recommends smoke and carbon monoxide alarms and monthly testing. Follow the alarm manufacturer's installation and replacement instructions and applicable local requirements.

Health Canada says to leave immediately for fresh air if a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, call emergency services from outside, and return only after professionals have resolved the problem. Do not stay inside to look for the source.

Budget for Care and Fuel as Well as Installation

Separate installation costs from fuel, tools, delivery, routine service, replacement components and repairs. Request current local quotes rather than extending an assumed yearly figure into a fifteen-year total.

Record the assumptions for occupancy, fuel use and service intervals. Update the budget from actual costs once the system is operating.

Keep the Operating Area and Records in Order

US Fire Administration guidance says to keep items that can burn at least three feet from heat sources. This housekeeping advice does not replace the appliance and venting clearances in the installation design.

EPA's archived practices advise placing ashes in a covered metal container outdoors on a nonflammable surface. Keep the manual, service contacts and operating instructions accessible to everyone responsible for the stove.

Common Questions

Can I use a generic clearance chart for my installation?

Use the exact appliance and venting-system instructions with local requirements. Do not assume a generic shielding reduction applies to the selected assembly.

Does annual chimney cleaning guarantee safe operation all year?

No. Arrange professional inspection and cleaning at least annually, and follow the appliance instructions and professional advice on additional checks or service.

Can I burn treated scrap wood in a stove?

Do not burn treated or painted wood, rubbish or glued wood products. Use fuel permitted by the appliance instructions.

What should I do if a carbon monoxide alarm sounds?

Leave immediately for fresh air. From outside, call local emergency services. Do not search for the source or return until professionals have resolved the problem.
